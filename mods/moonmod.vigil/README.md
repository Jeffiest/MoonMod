# Vigil (prototype S1)

Souls-lite combat for Chronicle's story game: slow, deliberate fights where you read an enemy, then guard or evade,
then punish. This is the first prototype. It uses only what the engine already offers. The plan, the findings behind
it and the engine work still to come are in [`VIGIL_PLAN.md`](../../VIGIL_PLAN.md).

## What it does

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **L2 held: run** | Hold L2 with the stick pushed and Toan runs, as fast as Dran's Feather used to make him, draining **20 weapon gauge a second** (5 seconds from full); the gauge doesn't refill while you run, and once it is empty you walk until you let go. So L2 rolls when you tap it, as you let it go. Holding L2 no longer swings the camera behind you. Dran's Feather is now a charm with a slot of its own, right of the amulet slot on the item page (a blacked-out Feather while it is empty): there it halves what running drains, using up a second of its time (shown on it, in seconds) for each second you run. *Run cost* in the settings sets the drain; 0 turns the run off and L2 evades on the press again. |
| **Lightness and Stamina** | The action gauge is split in two. Each hero refills at their own rate whatever weapon they hold, a second: Toan 35, Ungaga 30, Goro 60, Osmond 80, Xiao 120, Ruby 180 (Toan and Ungaga still refill nothing mid-swing). A weapon's **Speed** is now its *Lightness*: it sets what a swing costs, 60 / Speed of the usual cost, from half to double, so a quick dagger swings cheaper and a heavy club dearer, and the weapon cards label it **Lt** (Vigil redraws all four labels there, evenly spaced, one per bar). Rolls cost what they did. The *Lightness and Stamina* setting turns it off. |
| **Gear** | Hats, tunics, gloves, boots and pouches: each hero wears one of each. About one chest in five holds a piece, and now and then a monster drops one, common, fine, rare or (rarely) unique, rarer deeper in. A piece has one to three effects: a chance to shrug off an ailment before your amulet is spent (hats always have one), poise (tunics always, and only tunics: 1 to 4 points; a light hit spends 1 and a heavy one 2, and while it lasts a hit hurts but doesn't stagger you, so your swing or roll goes on; it comes back after two seconds without a hit), defence, cheaper swings (gloves), a cheaper run (boots), or more stamina. A pouch always grows the item bag, by 5 slots for a common one up to 20 for a unique (the biggest pouch anyone in the party wears counts, on top of the game's Pockets, up to the game's 100); take it off and the bag shrinks back as far as its items let. Each piece is the hero's own hat, vest, glove, shoe or bag, taken from their model (Toan's cap, Xiao's paw, Goro's bear mantle, Ruby's tiara and slippers, Ungaga's feathered cap, Osmond's goggles and bag), on the colour of what it does best (red boots run, a green hat guards against poison); a kind a hero has nothing for shows a drawn icon. Pieces go to the item page's **Equipment** tab (up to 99), and the item page shows your hero on his own, larger, with his amulet and Feather slots on the left, his weapon at his hand, four gear slots down his side and the pouch at his hip: pick a piece up from the tab and drop it on its slot (Cross or the mouse) to wear it, and drop a weapon from the Weapons tab on his hand to equip it. Square on a slot takes a piece off. Toan starts out wearing his own cap, vest and satchel (plain pieces with nothing to them), and Goro his mantle, Ungaga his cap and sash and Osmond his bag once they join. Each trip home every shop puts three pieces on its counter at twice their value (Triangle buys one, rolled as deep as your last dungeon), and on the shop's Equipment tab Triangle sells a piece for its value; with no hat on Toan goes bareheaded, with no tunic his vest comes off and with no pouch his satchel, and the others' come off the same way, on the item page and in the dungeon. The *Gear* setting turns it off, and the item page is the game's own again. |
| **L2: evade** | A roll the way the stick points, or a backstep with the stick at rest. Out of a run, Toan's roll pushes off whichever foot is leading. Locked on, with the stick to the side, he rolls sideways and keeps facing the enemy. The roll leaves the ground 0.11 s after the press, and the camera keeps up with it. Xiao, Ruby and Osmond tumble and hop back instead. You can't be hit while evading. Each evade costs **35 weapon gauge** and has a 30-frame cooldown. With the starting Dagger, four quick evades in a row empty the gauge, and L2 then waits for it to refill to 35. An evade can cut a swing short, but only 6 frames after its blow lands, so you commit to a swing. |
| **Toan's attacks** | On top of his combo: **R2** a heavy blow, or partway through the combo a finisher (after two blows, or three). Locked on, attack with the stick **toward** the enemy to lunge, or **away** to strike while backing off. Attack out of a run for a running attack, late in an evade for a follow-up, and while guarding for a counter. Each is its own motion file, a slot you can fill with your own (below). *Toan's attacks* in the settings turns them off. |
| **The weapon gauge is your stamina** | It's the gauge under your weapon at the bottom left. Dark Cloud already spends it on combo swings (a follow-up needs 20 and costs 25), and refills it at your weapon's Speed. |
| **Guard (R1)** | Hold R1 to guard, locked on or not. It covers only about 120° in front of you: a hit from the side or behind lands in full. Each blocked hit costs gauge (10, plus 1.5 for every point of damage it stops) and lets 15% of its damage through. A hit you can't pay for **breaks the guard**: it lands in full, the gauge empties and you're knocked down. Raise the guard just as a blow lands (within 6 frames of R1) to **parry**: no damage, no cost, and the attacker is stopped for a moment and shoved back, open to the Counter (attack while guarding). While you hold the guard, the gauge recovers at a quarter of its speed. *Souls guard* in the settings turns it back into the game's guard, which blocks everything from every side for free; the arc, costs, chip and parry window are settings too. |
| **Stamina that matters** | A hit that lands takes 15 gauge instead of filling it, the first swing of a combo costs 25 and needs at least 10, and the gauge waits 0.6 s after any spend before it refills. Mashing and trading hits empty you; backing off fills you. Empty it and you are **Exhausted** for 1.5 s: your swings deal 30% (the stamina badge blinks by the key plate). After a parry your next blow on that enemy within 2 s is a **riposte** at x3 (on any enemy of its kind, since the game can only aim a damage rule at a kind). *Souls stamina*, *Exhausted* and *Riposte* in the settings set or turn them off. |
| **Enemies fight smarter** | Most enemies that fight hand to hand circle at a distance and take turns: one attacks at a time. They sidestep, roll aside or step back from your swing and punish the whiff, feint (a wind-up broken off into a sidestep, then the real swing), rush in from the edge of the circle, string up to three blows, and hold a wind-up through your roll to hit you as it ends. A Skeleton Soldier often blocks your swing on its shield and strikes back; the others that have a guard in the game (Master Jacket, Werewolf, White Fang, Mummy, the Captain, Curse Dancer, Sunday, Monday and the card soldiers) use it the same way. The families so far: Dasher, Rash Dasher, Skeleton Soldier, Master Jacket, Werewolf, White Fang, Mummy, Captain, Corcea, Curse Dancer, Sunday, Monday, Wednesday, Saturday, Hell Pockle, Club, Diamond, Spade and Joker. Each has a new attack of its own now and then: the Skeleton's whirling slash (one full turn, blade out), the Dasher's and the Rash Dasher's pounce (a leap that slams the mace down in front) and the Werewolf's rake (a long leap, one claw raked down as it lands, then the other across). The rolls and attacks are Vigil's own motions (`resources/anims/e03a_*`, `e06a_*`, `e07a_*`, `e63a_*`, from `tools/anim/vigil_monster_moves.py`), added to the monsters by the engine. Fliers, shooters and casters keep their own AI for now. *Enemies fight smarter* in the settings turns it off, and turning it on takes over the monsters already on the floor. |
| **Weapon arts** | Guard (R1) and press R2 to use your weapon's art, once you have built it up to level 3. Toan's short blades (Dagger, Baselard, Gladius, the knives...) lunge and stab twice (*Piercing Rush*, 35 gauge), his long swords spin twice and cut (*Whirlwind*, 45), and his great swords (Buster Sword, Big Bang, Claymore...) slam the ground with a burst (*Earthsplitter*, 50). With *Party attacks*, the others have one each on all their weapons: Goro's *Earthquake* (a slow slam that shakes the ground around him), Ungaga's *Spear Storm* (two turns, then a thrust), Xiao's *Stone Rain* (three wide fans of stones), Ruby's *Inferno* (one great bursting orb) and Osmond's *Barrage* (three quick bursts). The weapon menu's card for each blade names its art and when it unlocks. *Weapon arts* and *Art level* are settings. |
| **The flask** | D-pad Down (or the *Flask key*) drinks from your flask: a slow drink that heals 40% of your max HP near its end. A hit that lands before then spills it, and the charge is gone, so drink out of reach or behind your guard. It holds 3 (the HUD shows `Flask 2/3`) and fills again when you rest at a shrine or go back to town. Now and then a chest holds a **Flask Shard** (one a floor at most): it adds a charge for good, up to 7 more. Bread, cheese and the other healing items refuse with an enemy near, from the item menu or a quick slot, so the flask is your heal in a fight; water and cures still work. *The flask*, its charges, its heal, the key and the distance are settings. |
| **Lock-on** | Locked on, flick the right stick left or right to move the lock to the next enemy that way. A target that goes behind a wall for half a second loses the lock. Not locked, a swing turns to the nearest enemy just ahead (within 35 degrees, the *Soft lock* setting). Each is a setting. |
| **Story bosses** | Dran and the bosses after him walk in with a card naming them, and their life runs along the bottom of the screen. At half life each one rages: a roar, a red glow, faster moves and harder blows until the fight ends. *Story bosses* is a setting. |
| **Named elites** | Each floor (each zone, with Vigil World) has one named foe, such as *Garrow the Unbowed*: gold, three times the life, half again the bite, five times the reward, with its own bar. When it falls it drops a piece of gear, the best of several rolls, and it stays down on later visits. *Named elites* and their strength are settings. |
| **Ambushes** | On a kept map, some zones are ambushes: a moment after you walk in, three of the floor's monsters drop in around you from above and land in a cloud of dust. Each happens once. The room shuts behind you until they are dead. *Ambushes*, their size and *Ambushers drop from above* are settings. |
| **Shortcuts** | On a kept map, a floor has up to two locked doors in dead ends, with the key in a chest beside them. Come the long way round, open one from the far side, and it stays open on every later visit. *Shortcuts* is a setting. |
| **Illusory walls** | In some dead ends, one wall is not a wall. Face it and swing: it fades, the way on opens, and something lies behind. It stays open. *Illusory walls* is a setting. |
| **Notes** | The first time you reach a shrine, a note left by someone who came before is scratched beside it: a warning, a tip or a line of lore. *Notes by the shrines* is a setting. |
| **NG+** | Once the Dark Genie falls, a shrine's *Leave* offers the **Next cycle**. Every map is made new: new floors, chests and elites, and shrines to light again. Monsters have half again the life, a quarter again the bite and half again the reward for each cycle. Your levels, tree and gear stay. *NG+* is a setting. |
| **Boss moves** | In its second phase each story boss has a move of its own: marks glint where it will land, and a moment later it falls there. Step off the mark, roll or guard. Each winds up in a motion of its own as the marks glint and strikes as they fall: Dran rears up with his wings spread, then slams his head down and breathes fire along the line; Utan springs and stamps, the Ice Queen calls frost, the King's Curse's coffin flies open to rain curses and slams shut, Joe leaps, the Genie sweeps a beam, and in his final form holds his charge and fires. *Boss moves* is a setting. |
| **Darkness and miasma** | In the Gallery of Time, a zone whose shrine you haven't lit lies in darkness. The first shrine you light there gives you the Shrine Lantern, a pouch: while anyone in the party wears it, the dark is a wide ring of warm light around you. In the Moon Sea, patches of foul mist poison you while you stand in them. *Darkness*, *The Shrine Lantern* and *Miasma* are settings. |
| **Your ghost** | Come back to where you fell and your ghost, pale and see-through, walks your last five seconds again. *Your ghost* is a setting. |
| **Elite patrols** | A named elite walks a beat around its part of the floor until you come near. *Elite patrols* is a setting. |
| **Ledges** | Beside some locked gates the floor falls away. Without the key you may drop down past the gate into the next zone, by its shrine, for a tenth of your life. There is no climbing back. *Ledges* is a setting. |
| **Messages** | At a shrine, the fifth card is Messages: leave one built from a phrase and a word ("Beware a mimic"), read the ones others left there, best rated first, and rate them good or poor. Reaching a shrine, the best rated one comes up. To trade messages with friends, everyone points their messages folder at one folder a sync service shares: put its full path on the first line of `save/moonmod/messages-folder.txt` (or set `DC_MESSAGES_DIR`). Anyone who has that folder reads what you leave. Without one, messages stay in your save. *Messages* is a setting. |
| **Height steps** | On a kept map, each zone lies a step below the one before: the corridor outside its gate runs down a ramp to it, so going deeper is going down. An ambush waits until you're off a ramp. *Each zone a step lower* is a setting. |
| **Invaders** | Some zones of a kept map hold an invader: a moment after you walk in, a dark copy of another party member steps out ahead of you, with a bar of its own, and comes for you. Guard, parry and dodge work on its blows, and every third swing is a slow, heavy one. It guards too: about two of your swings in five it meets with its own guard, and they clang off; strike as its guard comes down. Or it dashes aside out of your swing (never through a wall), and comes straight back at you. Between swings it circles you; lock on to it as you would a monster. Low on life it backs away and drinks: rush it and the flask is spilled, or it gets two fifths back (twice at most). Fell it for Gilda and gear; that zone stays clear. *Invaders* is a setting. |
| **Townsfolk** | Three folk are held in the dungeons, each by a named elite: a smith in the Cave, a trader in the Forest, a seeker in the Shipwreck. Fell the elite, walk up to them, and they go home. In town, D-pad Up (or H) calls them: the smith reinforces and mends your weapon, the trader sells gear, the seeker pays for a hunt. *Your town as a hub* is a setting. |
| **Vigil Trial** | A title-menu mode: pick a story boss (Dran to the Dark Genie) or one of six named elites (Garrow the Unbowed, a Skeleton Soldier, to Sable the Last Watch, a Club) and a hero, and fight it on an empty floor with Vigil's rules, the party armed for that point in the story. An elite is gold, bites half again as hard and has a bar of its own. Your best time for each is kept. *Vigil Trial* is a setting. |
| **YOU DIED** | A knockout shows the words before the fade. The dungeon map marks where you fell on your last eight deaths, as well as the pile of what you lost. *YOU DIED* is a setting. |
| **Criticals** | Attack at an enemy's back, or at one you have just parried or whose poise you broke, and Toan steps in and drives his sword through it: a stab of his own, at x3 on that enemy, while you can't be hit. Skeleton Soldiers and Dashers double over on the blade and stagger back up; the other families stand and take it. *Criticals* and *Critical damage* in the settings. |
| **Enemy poise** | An enemy shrugs off the first two blows of a flurry instead of flinching at each, so it can swing back at you; the third breaks its poise, and it flinches and staggers for two thirds of a second. A heavy blow (R2) or a combo finisher breaks it at once. Poise comes back after two seconds without a hit. Monsters with a brain carry on with what they were doing; other monsters lose the flinch but start their move again. *Enemy poise* (0 is the game's flinch at every hit) and *Stagger* in the settings. |
| **Target bar** | The enemy you lock on to shows a wider HP bar under its name, with a pale trail that holds what your last blows took and then drains, and under it a gold poise meter that fills with each blow and flashes white when its poise breaks. *Target bar* in the settings gives the game's thin gauge back. |
| **Hit feel** | A blow that lands holds the whole floor still for a moment: three frames for a light one, six for a heavy or charged one, ten as a critical begins. Heavy blows and getting hurt shake the camera, and the pad rumbles as blows land. *Hit feel* is a setting. |
| **Input buffer** | A roll or R2 pressed a little early, late in a swing, mid-roll or during a hit's freeze, is kept for 12 frames and comes out as soon as it can, instead of being lost. *Input buffer* is a setting (0 is the game's own). |
| **Telegraphs** | A monster's opening blow glints with a soft ping a beat before it lands. Its own new attack (the Skeleton's whirl, the Dasher's pounce, the Werewolf's rake) flashes red with a crack: roll, don't trade. *Telegraphs* is a setting. |
| **Shooters** | Monsters that shoot keep their distance: they back off when you close in, step aside, and stop to fire after a glint, never two at once. Close the gap or roll the shot. *Shooters* is a setting. |
| **Party attacks** | Xiao, Goro, Ruby, Ungaga and Osmond fight with more than their old combo: R2 is a heavy (a slam, a charged shot), R2 after two or three blows a finisher (a launcher, a spin, a fan or a volley of shots), and Goro and Ungaga counter from the guard and strike out of a roll. Each has a backstab and a riposte too, in a motion of its own: Goro slams his hammer down on the fallen enemy, Ungaga drives his spear in, Xiao, Ruby and Osmond press in and fire twice at point blank. *Party attacks* is a setting. |
| **Chain attacks** | Once a swing's blow has landed, R2 cuts its recovery short into the heavy; once the heavy's has, attack cuts back into the combo. Light, heavy, light flows without waiting out each swing. *Chain attacks* is a setting. |
| **Lock-on camera** | Locked on, the camera keeps the target in front of you instead of letting it drift to the edge of the screen, and pulls back for a big monster or a boss so you both fit. *Lock-on camera* is a setting. |
| **Enemies hit hard** | ×2.5 damage. |
| **Shorter hit stun** | A hit stuns you for 40% of the usual time. In Dark Cloud the stun is also your protection after a hit, so the next hit can land sooner. Don't get hit twice. |
| **Slower character switches** | ×2 the wait, so a switch isn't a free escape. |
| **Vigil World preview** (off by default) | Each floor is two zones on one map, joined by a locked gate. A monster in the first zone carries the gate's key: kill it, pick the key up and walk through, with no load. A gate once opened stays open on later visits, like an opened chest. Turn on *Vigil World preview* in the settings before a new game (it needs *The same floors every visit*): a save keeps the choice it first went into a dungeon with, so a story save already under way stays a normal Dark Cloud save, and one started with Vigil World keeps it. Each zone has a **shrine**, a spring against a wall: walk up to it and it lights; step into its water and press Square (E on the keyboard) for its panel. The water quenches thirst but doesn't heal. The panel offers *Rest* (heal the party and cure its ailments; the floor is built again with its monsters back, chests and gates as you left them, and you wake at the shrine), *Travel* (the dungeon's map opens with every other lit shrine to pick, on any floor; L1/R1 step between them, L2/R2 between maps, Cross goes), *Leave* (to the dungeon's town) and *Level up* (Circle stays). While the preview is on, a shrine is the only way back to town: the Escape menu refuses and the exit gate leads on. Walking in from town starts at the entrance, floor 1 (to start at a shrine, pick it on the town's map); the last shrine you reached is where you wake after a knockout (healed, the monsters back). The half of your Gilda a knockout takes, and your weapon's unspent ABS, lie where you fell, a pile of gold on the corner map and a mark on the map: walk up to it to take them back, before you fall again. A shrine's *Level up* opens the shrine tree on the Journal's Character page: spend Gilda on Vigour (max HP for the party), Endurance (the gauge refills faster) or Guard (guarding slows the refill less), only at a shrine. No monster starts near a shrine, and none steps into its circle (about two and a half cells around the spot in front of the spring): a chaser stops at the edge, so you wake and rest in peace. Monsters **scale** too (on by default with the preview, *Floor scaling*): each zone has a base level two steps deeper per floor, your level comes from your weapon's attack, and a monster is fought at whichever is higher of its zone's level and yours less two (*scaling gap*). A zone you have outgrown keeps up with you, with more life, harder hits and a little more reward, so nothing dies in a hit or two. Each dungeon becomes **one continuous map** too (*Regions*, on with the preview): every floor is a zone of it with its own monsters, atla and chests (Divine Beast Cave: floors 1-15; Gallery of Time is two maps, 1-12 and 13-25), and the entrance has no floor menu or loading card: the cave's door opens and you walk in, to floor 1, the village still in sight through the doorway behind you, with no walk down the stairs. The cave is there through the door the first time too, and the way out shows the village in daylight. Once the door has slid open it stays open: walk up to it facing in and you walk straight into the cave, no button. Matataki's forest path is connected the same way once you have walked into Wise Owl Forest (the forest isn't seen from the path before the first visit). The other towns' dungeons are reached from the world map, so they have no doorway. With Vigil World the towns lie along one **road**: walk out of a town's gate (where the world map used to open) and a panel asks which way, back or on to the next town (Norune, Matataki, Queens, Muska Lacka, Yellow Drops) or into the dungeons the world map reached (Shipwreck from Queens, Sun and Moon Temple from Muska Lacka, Moon Sea and Gallery of Time from Yellow Drops, each at floor 1); the trip is a cut, no loading card. *Stay* (or Circle) stays; walk off the gate and back to be asked again. Walk back out to the village and in again and floor 1 is as you left it, its monsters where they were. On a map whose first floor has the Fairy King's opening (Divine Beast Cave), he waits a few steps down the first corridor instead of the scene playing as you arrive: walk up to him and press Square to hear it. The story floors, where the game's own scenes and bosses are (Divine Beast Cave's 4, the skeleton; 8, Seda; each midway boss; and each last boss, such as Dran's 15), stand behind **smoke** (the floors that only lock the party to one character, such as Divine Beast Cave's 11 for Xiao, are ordinary zones): a gate no key opens, a wall of pale smoke in front of its door. Walk up to it and choose *Go through* to play that floor as the game has it; at its far gate (the one that wants the floor's key) choose *Leave* to come back to the map at the next floor's shrine, and the smoke is gone for good, its small zone a walk-through. Every arrival at a shrine (a rest, a wake, travel, the town's map, out of the smoke) is a teleport in the escape item's light, not a walk down the stairs. The back floor's key (Dran's Crest in Divine Beast Cave) drops from any monster on the map, once: it stays with the save through knockouts and trips to town (given back on your next visit until the back floor is entered), and a second one found pays 300 Gilda instead. The **Fairy King** stands beside the shrine of each zone whose floor has his scene (Divine Beast Cave's 3, 5 and 7) until you have heard it: walk up to him and press Square to talk, the scene plays, and you are back at that shrine afterwards. A map's exit gate leads past its last floor, entering by one of its later floors starts you at that floor's shrine, and the Floor number in the corner follows the zone you are in (`VIGIL_PLAN.md` section 9, W1.1 to W2). |
| **The map** | Select or L3 (M on the keyboard) in a dungeon pauses on the whole map of where you are, as far as you have explored it: rooms and roads, its shrines (lit or not) and locked gates once you have found them, and you. Move the cursor with the pad, jump between marks with L1/R1, and put a waypoint with Triangle; the minimap flags it too. What you have explored is kept with the floor, so it is still mapped next time. In the dungeon's town, L3 or M opens the maps of every place you have explored there (L2/R2 between them; Select stays the Georama button), and Cross on a lit shrine travels straight to it. On a map as big as a region, the minimap shows the part around you and scrolls as you walk. *Map screen* in the settings turns it off. |
| **The same floors every visit** | Each floor keeps the layout it had the first time you went down it, saved with your save slot, so a floor is a place you learn instead of a new maze each time. Its monsters come back, but a chest you opened stays opened (except the floor's map and magic crystal, which only work on the visit you find them). Back floors follow their floor. Demon Shaft stays new every time. *The same floors every visit* in the settings turns it off. This is the first step toward one continuous dungeon (`VIGIL_PLAN.md` section 9). |
| **A charm slot** | Each character has a slot of their own for an amulet on the item page, beside their weapon and shield (press Right from the shield to reach it); while it is empty it shows an amulet blacked out. An Antidote, Anti-Curse, Anti-Goo or Anti-Freeze Amulet there guards that character against its ailment just as from an active-item slot, using up a charge each time, so protection no longer costs one of your three quick slots, and an amulet in it takes no room in the bag. Only amulets go in it; Square puts it back in the bag. In the dungeon the amulet you wear shows at the top right, left of the key, with its uses left in the corner (the slot on the item page shows them too). A used amulet sells for less, and for nothing once it is down to under half its uses. It is kept with your save (beside it, as `charms.json`), and an amulet left in it stays shown even with Vigil off, so it is never lost. *Charm slot* in the settings turns it off. |
| **No Stamina power-up from enemies** | In this game the "Stamina" ailment is actually a power-up (double attack, defense and gauge refill), and some enemies hand it out. Vigil turns that off. |

Every number is a setting in the Mod Manager. **Check** on the mod's Actions page shows what's set right now.

Vigil sits out Dark Genie's Gauntlet. During a run it hands back L2, its tuning and its damage formula, and takes them
again afterwards.

## Playtest (gate S1)

Divine Beast Cave floors 1-3 as Toan, Vigil's defaults, nothing else gameplay-changing on.

- [ ] L2 with the stick pushed rolls that way; at rest, he backsteps. Neither can be hit.
- [ ] Holding L2 with the stick pushed runs, the gauge draining; empty, he walks until L2 is let go. A tap still rolls.
- [ ] Dran's Feather goes only in its own slot (right of the amulet's), and an amulet only in the amulet slot.
- [ ] Dran's Feather in its slot: running drains half as fast, and its seconds count down on the HUD.
- [ ] Lightness: a fast weapon's swings take less of the gauge, a slow one's more; Toan's dagger combo costs about 21 a swing, not 25.
- [ ] Stamina: each hero refills at their own rate whatever they hold (Ruby fastest, Ungaga slowest); turning *Lightness and Stamina* off brings the old refill back.
- [ ] Gear: open chests until a piece turns up (about one in five); it is in the item page's Equipment tab, tinted; drop it on the hero page's slot for its kind and the help box shows what it does.
- [ ] The hero page: Toan larger with no card, HP and water at his feet; drop a weapon from the Weapons tab on his hand and he equips it; the mouse drags pieces and weapons too.
- [ ] Gear works: a poison guard stops some poisonings without spending the amulet, defence takes hits down, boots make the run last longer.
- [ ] Gear stays: save, quit and load, and every hero still wears what they wore; the trash can throws a piece away.
- [ ] Locked on, L2 with the stick to the side rolls sideways, still facing the enemy.

## Your own moveset for Toan

Each of Toan's attacks plays a motion in `resources/anims/` (`toan_heavy`, `toan_finisher_2`, `toan_finisher_3`,
`toan_run_attack`, `toan_lunge`, `toan_back_attack`, `toan_roll_attack`, `toan_guard_counter`). The ones shipped are
copies of the Gauntlet's hand-made attacks. If you own Elden Ring, `tools/anim/reference/import_motion.py --moveset`
swaps a whole set of its attacks onto these slots in **your save's copy** of Vigil, never the mod as shipped. Each
attack's blow is lined up with the slot's own, so the hits still land on the swing. `--restore` puts the shipped
motions back. See [`tools/anim/reference/README.md`](../../tools/anim/reference/README.md).
